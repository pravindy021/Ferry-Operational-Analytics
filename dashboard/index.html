Data quality & methodology

Source observations and forecasting choices, with operational limitations made explicit.

SOURCE OBSERVATIONS—Rows retained after validation
MISSING 15-MIN SLOTS—Not interpolated or assumed to be zero
DUPLICATE TIMESTAMP ROWS—Same-time counts aggregated and reported
INVALID / NEGATIVE COUNTS—Excluded from forecasting

Forecasting design

  • 15-minute grid; missing slots remain missing and are excluded from labels.
  • Lag, rolling, and calendar features use only data available at the forecast origin.
  • Direct models predict each target at 15, 30, 60, and 120 minutes.
  • Naive, moving-average, daily/weekly seasonal naive, linear regression, and histogram gradient boosting are compared.
  • Chronological 70/15/15 train-validation-test split. Best model is selected on validation MAE; test data is never used for selection.
  • Prediction intervals use horizon-specific validation residual quantiles (80% and 95%); they are not model confidence scores.

Operational limitations

  • Data does not include sailing schedules, passenger counts, vessel capacity, occupancy, weather, or service disruptions.
  • Demand classifications are relative to historical P50/P75/P90 values, not safety or capacity limits.
  • Missing intervals may reflect operating patterns or data collection gaps; their causes are unknown.
  • Forecasts support monitoring and readiness review only; they do not prescribe exact departures or capacity.
  • Historical records end at the timestamp shown above. A forecast is anchored to the latest observation, not the computer’s current time.
